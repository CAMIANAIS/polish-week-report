## Part 1: SUNAT audits

### Question 1 — How many audits, inspections, induction actions, notices, and verifications does SUNAT report?

**Answer:** The latest official year located is **2025**. For domestic taxes, SUNAT reported **181,184 control actions**; the aggregate includes definitive and partial audits/inspections, electronic partial audits/inspections, and induction actions.[1] Within that same report there were **9,447 determinative inspection actions**, but SUNAT does not separate them into definitive, partial, and electronic partial inspections; therefore, those three annual counts are **unknown**.[1] Electronic partial inspection (FPE) is conducted from offices, begins with a preliminary assessment, grants 10 business days to remedy or submit a response, and has a maximum duration of 30 business days; SUNAT states that there is no electronic definitive inspection.[2][3] In 2025, **19 FPE pilot actions** were reported, not necessarily the complete annual total.[1][3]

SUNAT reported **50,603 electronic case files generated for “Induction Actions”** in 2025, compared with 41,185 in 2024; this figure must retain that definition and must not automatically be converted into a number of letters or notices.[1] An induction action is a communication about differences detected between returns and third-party data, aimed at voluntary regularization and not equivalent to a determinative inspection.[4][5] The report does not separate 2025 into induction letters and notices; both counts are **unknown**.[1] As a historical definition, the letter normally does not require attendance at an office, while the notice does require attention at a service center or office; the protocol defining that distinction is from **September 2014, before 2022**.[5]

The report also recorded **118,517 verification actions**, separate from the aggregate of 181,184; 181,184 + 118,517 = 299,701, but this must not be presented as a single audit count or as unique cases.[1] In different universes, it reported **51,009 inspection actions and labor inspections** and **990 post-clearance customs controls** —205 audits, 526 inspections, and 259 verifications—; these must not be added to domestic taxes.[1]

**Year:** 2025 for the main counts; 2024 for the comparison; 2014 for the historical definition of letter/notice (marked as before 2022).

### Question 2 — What proportion of taxpayers, MYPE, or RMT is audited?

**Answer:** The annual proportion of **unique taxpayers audited** is not published. SUNAT reported **196,804 domestic-tax control actions** in 2024 and **12,532.2 thousand registered taxpayers** as of December 2024; the mechanical division 196,804 / 12,532,200 = **1.5704%**, but this is not an audit rate because the numerator consists of actions, not unique people or RUCs, and also includes induction actions.[6] For 2025, 181,184 actions and 12,962.5 thousand registered taxpayers produce 181,184 / 12,962,500 = **1.3978%**, with the same limitation.[1]

SUNAT reported **1,080.9 thousand RMT registrants** as of December 2024 and **940.5 thousand** as of December 2025, but it did not publish a specific numerator for audited MYPE/RMT taxpayers; the MYPE/RMT rate is therefore **unknown**.[6][8] RMT is also not equivalent to all MYPE: it is a tax regime with its own rules and a net-income limit of 1,700 UIT.[8] The OECD provides denominators of active taxpayers by tax type for Peru in fiscal year 2023 —PIT 6.83 million, CIT 1.45 million, and VAT 1.31 million—, but no numerator of audited taxpayers or coverage rate; these universes must not be added together as unique taxpayers.[9]

**Year:** 2024–2025 for SUNAT counts; 2023 for the OECD context; the RMT source contains a legal rule from before 2022, used only to define the regime.

### Question 3 — How many audits focus on IGV or tax credit?

**Answer:** The number and percentage of audits specifically focused on IGV or tax credit are **unknown/not published**. The 2025 report records 181,184 control actions, 9,447 determinative actions, and 118,517 verifications, but does not classify those universes by tax or topic; none should be presented as IGV audits.[1] As separate context, the Electronic IGV Compliance Control Model issued **1,227,886 alert messages** in 2025, and **3,882 preventive IGV messages** were recorded within 582,480 messages to the SOL Inbox, approximately 0.67%; these are alerts or preventive messages, not audits.[1][10]

### Question 4 — What are the tax limitation periods?

**Answer:** Article 43 of the Tax Code establishes **4 years** to determine the tax obligation, demand its payment, and apply penalties; **6 years** if the relevant return was not filed; and **10 years** when a withholding or collection agent did not pay the tax withheld or collected. The action to request or make an offset and to request a refund expires after 4 years.[14] SUNAT Report 072-2016 interprets the 10-year period as operating regardless of whether the return was filed and the 6-year period as not automatically extending to someone who had no legal obligation to file.[16]

Article 44 establishes when the period begins: depending on the case, on January 1 of the year following the due date of the annual return, on the date the obligation becomes enforceable or arises, on the date of the violation or its detection, on the date of the undue/excess payment, or on the date the refundable credit arises; to collect a debt contained in an assessment resolution or fine, it begins on the day after notification.[14][15] Articles 45 and 46 may interrupt or suspend the calculation, so a final date requires reviewing the specific facts and notifications.[14][18]

**Year:** Consolidated text consulted with amendments through **26-06-2026**; the interpretive reports from 2003, 2006, and 2016 predate 2022 and are identified as interpretations, not substitutes for the statutory text.

**Source:** [14] [SUNAT, TUO del Código Tributario, texto completo](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/codigo/textoCompleto-TUO-CT.pdf) (official: yes); [15] [SUNAT, Prescripción — Empresas](https://orientacion.sunat.gob.pe/3781-prescripcion-empresas) (official: yes); [16] [SUNAT, Informe 072-2016-SUNAT/5D0000](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/oficios/2016/informe-oficios/i072-2016.pdf) (official: yes, older than 2022); [17] [SUNAT, Informe 355-2003-SUNAT/2B0000](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/oficios/2003/oficios/i3552003.htm) (official: yes, older than 2022); [18] [SUNAT, Informe 287-2006-SUNAT/2B0000](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/oficios/2006/oficios/i2872006.htm) (official: yes, older than 2022)

### Question 5 — How long must invoices, books, and records be retained?

**Answer:** Article 87(7) requires books, records, documents, and operating background capable of generating tax obligations to be stored, archived, and retained for **5 years or the applicable limitation period, whichever is longer**; Article 87(8) imposes a parallel obligation for programs, magnetic media, and electronic storage media, also for the longer period.[19] Retention is not simply “4 years”: Article 43 sets periods of 4, 6, or 10 years depending on the circumstance, and Articles 44–46 govern commencement, interruption, and suspension; Article 46(1)(e) suspends the limitation period while SUNAT allows books and records to be reconstructed.[20]

For electronic tax documents, SUNAT requires invoices, notes, rejections, daily summaries, and cancellation communications to be stored, archived, and retained; availability on the issuer’s website for **one year** is an access rule for the purchaser, not a replacement for the tax-retention duty.[25] Article 41 of the IGV Law links retention of invoices and accounting documents to the limitation period.[26] If books or records are lost or destroyed, Resolution 234-2006 requires SUNAT to be informed within 15 business days and allows 60 calendar days to recreate them, with a possible justified extension; that resolution and Reports 162-2006 are **before 2022**.[23][24]

No single special “invoice” duration was found that displaces the longer period under Article 87 for all taxpayers. The 2019 conclusion about eliminating originals after legally compliant microforms refers to regulated microforms, not to any ordinary PDF or scan.[22] Legislative Decree 1536 of 2022 contains a special rule for extinguished companies, but if Article 87 also applies, the longer period controls.[27]

**Year:** Rule in force in the consolidated Code consulted in 2026; CPE guidance amended in 2025; Resolution 234-2006 and Report 162-2006 predate 2022 and are marked as background/historical procedure.

**Source:** [19] [SUNAT, Código Tributario, Libro Segundo, art. 87](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/codigo/libro2/libro.pdf) (official: yes); [20] [SUNAT, Código Tributario, Libro Primero, arts. 43–46](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/codigo/libro1/libro.pdf) (official: yes); [21] [El Peruano, Decreto Legislativo 1315](https://busquedas.elperuano.pe/api/archivo/file/D8HtHaPw43G8H_MmabcHXR/*/1469407-4.pdf) (official: yes, older than 2022); [22] [SUNAT, Informe 023-2019-SUNAT/7T0000](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/oficios/2019/informe-oficios/i023-2019-7T0000.pdf) (official: yes); [23] [SUNAT, Informe 162-2006-SUNAT/2B0000](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/oficios/2006/oficios/i1622006.htm) (official: yes, older than 2022); [24] [SUNAT, Resolución 234-2006/SUNAT](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/superin/2006/234.htm) (official: yes, older than 2022); [25] [SUNAT CPE, conservación y puesta a disposición](https://cpe.sunat.gob.pe/sistema_emision/see_contribuyente) (official: yes); [26] [SUNAT, TUO Ley IGV e ISC, art. 41](https://www.sunat.gob.pe/legislacion/igv/ley/capitu10.htm) (official: yes); [27] [El Peruano, Decreto Legislativo 1536](https://busquedas.elperuano.pe/dispositivo/NL/2051320-1) (official: yes)

### Question 6 — What are the common reasons for rejection or disallowance of tax credit?

**Answer:** There is no official statistic that makes it possible to rank the reasons by frequency; the following is a list of regulatory risks and controls described by SUNAT, not a prevalence rate.[13][33] Substantive requirements include: IGV separately stated or paid on importation, an acquisition acceptable as a cost/expense, and use for taxable operations.[13] Formal requirements include an invoice with IGV separately stated, a verifiable issuer name and RUC, an issuer authorized as of the relevant date, and entry in the Purchase Register in accordance with the applicable rule.[13][29]

The contemplated disallowances or exclusions include a false or unreliable invoice; an issuer that has been deregistered or is “not located”; an issuer that is not an IGV taxpayer or whose regime does not allow credit; an exempt transaction; an unsupported invoice or one entered after the deadline; a sales slip used as credit; a rejected CPE; and a non-genuine transaction.[13][29][33][34] SUNAT states that a “Accepted” CDR has tax validity, “Accepted with observation” retains validity but may generate disallowances, and “Rejected” lacks tax validity and requires a new issuance.[35][36]

A non-genuine transaction may be nonexistent/simulated or real but carried out by a party different from the issuer; credit can be retained in the second situation only if the payment methods, identity of the goods/service, and other legal requirements are met, without knowledge of the true identity when contracting.[33] A positive RUC or CPE query helps prove identity and validity, but by itself does not prove reality, deductibility, or use for taxable operations.[37][38]

The SUNAT guide showing a historical window of up to 12 months for entering invoices is undated and predates the amendment of Article 2 of Law 29215 by Legislative Decree 1669 of 2024; for current periods, the applicable regime, SIRE/RCE, PLE, or other applicable method must be verified.[12][30][31][32] Report 230-2005 is **before 2022** and serves only as historical interpretation.[41]

**Year:** Rules consulted as of 14-10-2026; relevant changes from 2022–2025; the historical guide and Report 230-2005 are marked as earlier or potentially outdated.

## Part 2: Running costs

### Question 7 — What is the average monthly number of purchase invoices per MYPE?

**Answer:** The average monthly number of purchase invoices per Peruvian microenterprise or small enterprise is **unknown/not published**. PRODUCE reports for 2023 the formal operating universe —2,308,537 companies, 99.4% Mipyme, 2,168,708 microenterprises, and 121,070 small enterprises— and their sales limits, but not a count of invoices received per month.[42] EMYPE 2012 likewise does not measure that volume: it records knowledge of factoring and annual monetary purchase categories, not a monthly number of tax documents.[46][47][48] A 2023 study based on 384 Lima MSE accountants, with a non-probability sample and fieldwork in 2021, reports 45.3% use of electronic purchase invoices, but not monthly quantities or a national distribution.[49]

SUNAT describes the RCE as making a proposal of received tax documents available from the second calendar day of each month and updating it daily; this is a registration process, not an observed average per MYPE.[43] Annual sales, number of companies, electronic adoption, or number of records must not be converted into an estimated monthly volume.

### Question 8 — How much does it cost to store electronic invoices?

**Answer:** There is no typical official Peruvian size for XML, PDF, CDR, attachments, or versions; SUNAT publishes structures, fields, and packaging rules, not an average number of bytes per invoice.[54][55][56][57] For planning, AWS S3 Standard in US East publishes **US$0.023/GB-month** for the first 50 TB; Google Cloud Storage regional in Santiago publishes **US$0.030/GiB-month** for Standard, US$0.018 Nearline, US$0.006 Coldline, and US$0.0027 Archive.[50][52] AWS and GCS use different units —decimal GB versus binary GiB— and separate charges may apply for requests, retrieval, transfer, transitions, minimums, and monitoring.[50][52]

If `N` is the monthly number of invoices, `S` the average total stored size per invoice in bytes, `R` the number of copies, and `M` the accumulated retention in months, approximate accumulated storage is:

`Stored_GiB = N × M × S × R / 2^30`  
`Cost_storage_GCS_Santiago = Stored_GiB × US$0,030`  
`Cost_storage_AWS_US_East = (N × M × S × R / 10^9) × US$0,023`

These formulas are for planning and do not constitute a numerical estimate for Aldía because `N`, `S`, `M`, and `R` were not provided or verified. The tax-retention rule may require the longer of five years and the applicable limitation period, so `M` must not be set without each client’s legal profile.[19][20]

**Year:** Cloud prices consulted in 2026; SUNAT structure/packaging rules from 2021–2022 and XML guide from 2017, the latter two before 2022. Prices are USD, before taxes, FX, discounts, and excluded charges.

### Question 9 — How much does invoice OCR or data extraction cost?

**Answer:** The public prices consulted are provider prices, not SUNAT rates. AWS Textract charges **US$0.0015 per page** for Detect Document Text in the first monthly million and **US$0.01 per page** for Analyze Expense in the first million; the examples correspond to US West (Oregon).[58] Google Document AI Invoice Parser charges **US$0.10 per count of up to 10 pages**: a single page may cost US$0.10 and a 10-page document is equivalent to US$0.01 per page; the charge is not linear per page.[60] Azure Document Intelligence prebuilt Invoice publishes **US$10 per 1,000 pages = US$0.01/page** on pay-as-you-go/S0 and an F0 tier of 0–500 pages free per month.[62]

For `N` invoices, `P` pages per invoice, and `q` OCR executions per page, the formulas are:

`AWS_Detect = N × P × q × US$0,0015`  
`AWS_Analyze_Expense = N × P × q × US$0,01`  
`Azure_Invoice = N × P × q × US$0,01`  
`Google_Invoice_Parser = N × ceil(P / 10) × q × US$0,10`

The amounts above exclude taxes, network, storage, additional calls, contractual discounts, and conversion to soles.[58][60][62] Since `N`, `P`, and `q` are not verified, Aldía’s OCR cost is **numerically unknown**.

### Question 10 — How do RUC/CPE queries, APIs, and their costs work?

**Answer:** The human RUC Query is a form that allows searching by RUC, document, or name/business name and requires entering the displayed code; the multiple query allows up to 10 RUCs per submission. SUNAT also offers a downloadable reduced register with RUC, name, status, address condition, and location; this is the documented mass-query route, not an anonymous API.[38][64][65][66] No official fee, numerical request quota, or express authorization to automate the human forms through scraping was found.[38][64]

The human CPE validation requests the issuer’s RUC, document type and number, recipient identity, date, and amount.[37][69] SUNAT also documents an official authenticated API, `POST https://api.sunat.gob.pe/v1/contribuyente/contribuyentes/RUC/validarcomprobante`, with a Bearer token and document fields; it requires SOL, application registration, `client_id`/`client_secret`, and OAuth, so it is official but not anonymous.[71][72] The manual for that API is **before 2022**; the endpoint and credentials should be verified before production.[72]

SIRE offers access through SOL, a downloadable client, and an authenticated Web API; its 2024–2025 manuals document RCE/RVIE proposals, tickets, and files, as well as a ZIP restriction of up to 6 GB for relevant uploads. The documentation states that SIRE API services must not be consumed from a web client and does not publish a numerical requests/minute limit, concurrency limit, or monthly quota.[40][73][74][75] SUNAT does not publish a price for the RUC Query, reduced register, CPE validation, or SIRE. Third-party values —for example, Perú API quotas or S/40/month from NubeFact— are not SUNAT rates and are not used for the official cost.[76][77]

### Question 11 — What data-protection obligations apply to an invoice SaaS platform?

**Answer:** Law 29733 protects the personal data of identified or identifiable natural persons and considers collection, storage, consultation, use, and transfer to be processing.[78][79] The current Regulation is D.S. 016-2024-JUS, published on 30-11-2024 and in force since 31-03-2025; it repealed the previous regulation.[80][81][82] The database must be registered in the RNPD except for the strictly personal/family-use exception; registration is free and must be kept up to date.[80]

The controller decides the purposes and means; the processor processes on behalf of and under the instructions of the controller.[80] A SaaS that only processes invoices according to the client’s instructions may qualify as a processor, but the classification depends on the contract, sub-processors, and any reuse of data. Consent must be free, prior, express, unequivocal, and informed when no legal exception applies, and the notice must state the purposes, recipients, transfers, retention, and rights.[78][80][85]

The platform must apply proportionate technical, organizational, and legal measures, access controls and authentication, privilege reviews at least every six months, an inventory and security document, interaction logs for at least two years, secure backups at least weekly when updates are made, and safeguards for electronic transfers.[80] An incident meeting the regulatory conditions —large volume, many people, sensitive data, or evident harm— must be reported to the ANPD within 48 hours; the processor must immediately notify the controller.[80]

Foreign cloud services may constitute a cross-border flow and require an adequate level or safeguards, in addition to the required communication/registration.[78][80] The processor may not use data for another purpose; when the service ends, it must return or delete the data, and the regulation sets a maximum retention period of two years from the last assignment as the rule, unless there is authorization or a longer legal obligation.[80] This does not replace tax retention: the tax retention of invoices and books may be longer.[19][25][84] Rights include information, access, rectification, cancellation, objection, and, subject to conditions, portability; response periods are 8 days for information, 20 for access, and 10 for rectification, cancellation, or objection, with possible justified extensions.[80]

**Year:** 2011 Law and subsequent compilations; current Regulation published in 2024 and effective in 2025; undated SUNAT electronic-retention guidance. The compilation incorporating the repealed regulation predates 2022 and does not control current regulatory rules.

## Cost estimate

**Scope:** one accountant, 37 clients

**Answer:** The volume of purchase invoices and the size of each file have not been verified, and neither the accountant’s salary, fee, nor hours were provided. As required by the supplied results, **do not guess**: the total monthly amount is **unknown**.

Use `N` = total monthly invoices for the 37 clients; `P` = pages per invoice; `S` = bytes stored per invoice (XML + CDR + PDF + attachments + versions); `R` = copies/replication; `M` = retention months; `L_RUC` = RUC queries; `L_CPE` = CPE validations; `q` = number of OCR executions; `A` = the accountant’s monthly cost in soles. The size `S` must be measured from a real sample of files because SUNAT does not publish a typical size.[54][55][56][57]

**Storage:**

`GiB_acumulados = N × M × S × R / 2^30`

`Costo_GCS_Santiago_USD = GiB_acumulados × 0,030`

`Costo_AWS_US_East_USD = (N × M × S × R / 10^9) × 0,023`

The reference rates are Google Cloud Storage Standard Santiago US$0.030/GiB-month and AWS S3 Standard US East US$0.023/GB-month; requests, retrieval, transfers, minimums, taxes, exchange rate, and discounts should be added only if contracted.[50][52]

**OCR:**

`Costo_AWS_Detect_USD = N × P × q × 0,0015`

`Costo_AWS_AnalyzeExpense_USD = N × P × q × 0,01`

`Costo_Azure_Invoice_USD = N × P × q × 0,01`

`Costo_Google_InvoiceParser_USD = N × ceil(P/10) × q × 0,10`

These are public prices per page or per block of up to 10 pages, depending on the provider; they do not include the unquantified items listed above.[58][60][62]

**SUNAT/RUC:** The human RUC query, reduced register, and CPE/API validation have no official published fee in the sources reviewed; this **must not automatically be modeled as a guaranteed price of S/0**, but as “official price not published.”[38][64][65][71][72] If an authenticated API of one’s own is used, the published SUNAT cost is `unknown`; if a third party is engaged, its contractual fee must be added without confusing it with a SUNAT fee.[76][77]

`Costo_lookup = C_RUC × L_RUC + C_CPE × L_CPE`

with `C_RUC` and `C_CPE` unknown in the absence of a contract or official fee. If the control requires one query per invoice, then `L_CPE = N`; if it requires one query per supplier/RUC per month, `L_RUC` depends on the number of suppliers, which was also not provided. These quantities must not be fixed by assumption.

**Total monthly operating cost:**

`Total_S/mes = A + FX × (Costo_storage_USD + Costo_OCR_USD) + Costo_lookup + otros costos`

`FX` and `A` were not supplied; neither were `N`, `S`, `P`, `M`, `R`, `q`, `L_RUC`, `L_CPE`, lookup fees, or third-party costs. Therefore, the numerical total for **one accountant and 37 clients is unknown**. If “S/5/RUC/month” is an operating ceiling, 37 RUC × S/5 = **S/185/month**; that is only the arithmetic target budget, not validation that the service can cover the accountant, OCR, storage, and queries within S/185.

**Year:** Cloud/OCR costs and access documentation consulted in 2026; SUNAT technical structure from 2022 and earlier manuals identified where applicable. There is no verified official volume with which to turn the formulas into an amount.
